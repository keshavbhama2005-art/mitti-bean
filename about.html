Since 2019

A cafe built around one slow cup.

Mitti & Bean started as a single kulhad stall outside a Gurugram metro station. Six years later, it's still run by the same family — just with better chairs.

The Founder

"My grandmother made filter coffee the same way for forty years — same steel filter, same three-minute wait. I didn't want to modernize that. I wanted to build a room worthy of it."

Devika RaoFounder & Head Roaster

Devika grew up between Bengaluru and her family's coffee estate in Chikmagalur, where mornings started with her grandmother's steel filter and ended with the whole street stopping by for a cup. She moved to Gurugram in 2018 for a corporate job she didn't love, and left it a year later to open a kulhad stall with a roaster she'd built herself from a modified drum. Mitti & Bean — "mitti" meaning soil, or earth — is named for the clay cup and the ground the beans come from.

The Bean's Journey

From the estate in Chikmagalur to your table on Golf Course Road.

1
November – January

Hand-picked at the estate

Ripe cherries are picked by hand across two family-run estates in Chikmagalur and Coorg — Devika still visits both during harvest.

2
Within 48 hours

Sun-dried and milled

Cherries are pulped, fermented, and sun-dried on raised beds before the green beans are milled and graded by size.

3
Twice a week

Roasted in-house, in small batches

Every batch is roasted on-site in our 5kg drum roaster — small enough that Devika still tastes-tests each one by hand.

4
To order

Brewed the moment you sit down

No batch-brewed carafes sitting on a burner. Filter coffee is pulled fresh, kulhad by kulhad.

What We Won't Compromise On

A few things we've kept since the metro-station stall

Compostable kulhads, always

Every kulhad is hand-thrown by a family of potters in Faridabad and returns to the earth after one use — no glaze, no plastic lining.

Direct trade, no middlemen

We buy straight from the two estates we know personally, at a price fixed above market — regardless of what the commodity rate does.

Nothing sits longer than 8 weeks

We roast to the volume we actually sell. If a lot is running low, it comes off the menu rather than getting stretched.
